# Matte v1.1 — fix attempt for the 5 diagnosed failure modes

This documents an honest attempt to fix the 5 failure modes `MATTE_V1_BENCHMARK_REPORT.md` found in the v1.0.0 baseline. **Four of the five attempted mitigations measurably failed or made things worse and were reverted. One measurably worked and is kept.** This document exists so that history isn't lost — the reverted attempts and the real reasons they didn't work are recorded here rather than silently discarded, in case a better-informed future attempt wants to avoid repeating them.

## What shipped

**No-subject noise floor** (`src/lib/matting/pipeline/remove-background.ts`): inside the pre-existing "unusual foreground coverage" branch (`coverageFraction < 0.02 || > 0.98` — i.e. only when the pipeline is already about to warn that this may be a failed segmentation), residual alpha below 0.1 is now snapped to exactly 0, instead of being left as a diffuse low-confidence haze spread across the frame.

**Verified result** (`MATTE_V1.1_FINAL_VERIFICATION.md`, raw data in `benchmark-artifacts/v1.1-final/`): classification counts improved from v1.0.0's 17 PASS / 8 ACCEPTABLE / 8 NEEDS_REFINEMENT / 1 FAIL to **18 / 8 / 7 / 1** — `hard-no-subject` moved NEEDS_REFINEMENT → PASS (MAD 0.0351 → 0.0023, ~15x). All 23 other image cases and all 9 video cases are exact matches to the baseline, with one disclosed exception: `hard-smoke-like-regions` (the only other case whose coverage also trips the same pre-existing branch) saw its MAD tick up very slightly (0.0843 → 0.0879) with **no classification change** — flooring removed a few pixels that were a partial, honest match to that case's genuinely-diffuse low ground truth. Net effect: one real improvement, one negligible and disclosed side effect, nothing else touched.

## What was attempted and reverted, and why

The first implementation pass (plan approved, all 6 pieces implemented, 448 unit tests + tsc + build all green) looked complete on paper. Live re-verification against the full 34-case benchmark told a different story: **classification counts went from 17/8/8/1 to 13/12/8/1 — a net regression, with zero cases improving.** Full data in `benchmark-artifacts/v1.1/` and `MATTE_V1.1_BENCHMARK_COMPARISON.md`. Per-piece:

- **Feather (softened the trimap Unknown band, radius 2).** Intended to help transparency-class cases (glass, sheer fabric, smoke) by widening the soft-alpha transition. Measured: barely moved the target case (`hard-semi-transparent-fabric` MAD 0.1008 → 0.1001, noise-level) while nudging 5 previously-PASS cases into ACCEPTABLE. Root cause found only after measuring: that case's ground truth is semi-transparent across the *entire subject interior* (a row-based 0.3–0.8 alpha ramp over the whole silhouette — see `scene-builder.ts`'s `makeGradientTransparent`), not just its boundary. U²-Net's raw output classifies most of that interior as confident FOREGROUND (trimap Unknown only covers the outer edge ring), so Unknown-band-only feathering structurally cannot touch the region where most of the error actually lives. **Reverted.**
- **Hard/binary-edge warning** (`softEdgeFraction < 0.15 AND edgeGradientEnergy > 0.3`). Measured: fired on 0 of 25 cases, including the FAIL case it targeted — feather (above) had already pushed every case's `softEdgeFraction` uniformly higher, erasing the exact signature the warning was designed to detect. The two sub-fixes undermined each other. **Reverted along with feather.**
- **Automatic `HairRefinementEngine` wiring**, gated on a `detectHairCandidates` pixel-count pre-check. Measured: the gate (tuned against 64×64 adversarial test fixtures) fires on essentially every real 480×480 image — an ordinary product-shot edge alone produces 6,000+ candidate pixels, no more selective for hair than for any other edge. It provided zero benefit even on its own target case (`hard-curly-hair` MAD 0.0760 → 0.0764, marginally worse) while spiking `hard-fur`'s connectivity error 13x (0.0146 → 0.1901) and regressing several previously-PASS cases. An interim attempt to gate it additionally on source-image texture (`sourceSharpness > 0`) fixed a real crash-adjacent regression found in the adversarial test suite (it was shrinking small real subjects on flat-color test fixtures) but did not fix the broader over-triggering problem. **Reverted.**
- **Source-image blur warning** (`computeSourceSharpness < 8`, mean whole-frame Sobel luminance-gradient energy). Measured: fired on 20 of 25 cases (80%), including the 3 sharpest cases in the dataset, while correctly firing on the true motion-blur case only by coincidence. Root cause: a whole-frame mean can't distinguish "genuinely blurry" from "sharp but mostly flat/plain background" — both produce a low frame-wide average, and most of this benchmark's Easy/Medium cases are exactly that (a plain-background studio-style shot). **Reverted, including the underlying `computeSourceSharpness` function** (nothing else consumed it once this and the hair-gate were both reverted).
- **Fragmentation warning** (`remainingComponentCount > 5`, a new field threaded up from `refine/cleanup.ts`'s existing connected-component labeling). Measured: fired on ordinary hair/fine-detail cases (`medium-person-with-hair`, `hard-fine-hair`) instead of its target (`hard-smoke-like-regions`, which the no-subject floor had already suppressed to near-empty first). Component *count* doesn't correlate with "diffuse/incoherent vs. solid subject" the way connectivity error (measured against ground truth, unavailable in production) does — real hair/fur legitimately produces multiple small components too. **The warning text was reverted; the underlying `remainingComponentCount` field in `CleanupResult`/`RefineAlphaMatteResult` was kept** (it's real, accurate, already unit-tested, harmless, and logged for diagnostic visibility — just not surfaced as a user-facing warning that doesn't discriminate correctly).
- **Lightweight temporal smoothing for video** (`video-cutout.ts`, motion-weighted EMA blend between consecutive sparse-sampled frames, reusing `temporal-matting-engine.ts`'s scene-cut threshold and blend formula without its occlusion-hold extension). Measured: produced **zero** change in `temporalConsistencyError` on any of the 9 video cases, including the target (`video-fast-movement`, 0.0200 → 0.0200 exactly). Root cause: the blend weight is deliberately derived from local motion and collapses toward 0 exactly when motion is high — which is definitionally true for "fast movement," the one case that needed help. It did correctly avoid smearing `video-scene-cut`, but provided no offsetting benefit anywhere. **Reverted** (deleted `video-temporal-smoothing.ts` and its tests; `video-cutout.ts` restored to its original form).

## What remains genuinely unfixed

- **True semi-transparency (glass, sheer fabric, smoke) is not modeled.** This is the root cause of both failure #1 and failure #2, and no mitigation attempted here — nor, on reflection, any mitigation achievable without a different model — actually addresses it. U²-Net's binary segmentation has no soft-alpha concept; fixing this for real would mean bundling a genuine matting model (see `MATTE_IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md`'s MODNet/BackgroundMattingV2 adapters, built but never given weight files — `MATTE_V1_BASELINE.md`'s known limitations #the MODNet/BMv2 rows), not a post-processing patch.
- **Hair-type inconsistency (curly/voluminous vs. fine/straight) is unfixed.** `HairRefinementEngine` exists and is unit-tested but measurably does not improve this specific failure mode when applied automatically; it remains reachable only via the manual "Enhance hair detail" button, as before this work started.
- **Fast-motion video flicker is unfixed.** The one temporal-smoothing technique tried (motion-weighted EMA) is structurally unable to help on high-motion frames by construction. A real fix would need either a genuinely predictive/optical-flow-aware technique, or accepting the per-frame quality hit as the actual root cause (fast motion also degrades the still-image `hard-motion-blur`-style per-frame result, which is itself unfixed).
- **`hard-complex-background` ships with no warning**, as documented in the original plan — confidence, coverage, and histogram-bimodality all stay in normal ranges for this case; no ground-truth-free signal in the current metric toolkit distinguishes it from a good result. Unchanged from before this work.
- **Fragmentation on diffuse subjects (`hard-smoke-like-regions`, connectivity error 0.64) is unfixed** — no reliable production-side signal was found that flags this specifically without also misfiring on legitimate hair/fur texture.
